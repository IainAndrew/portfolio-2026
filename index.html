How I work

I treat coding agents the way a good tech lead treats a fast, tireless, slightly overconfident team. The thinking stays with me: what to build, how to split it, what done looks like. I write the plan first, in prose, with the interfaces and the failure modes spelled out, and then hand over one well-scoped piece at a time.

Agents write most of the code. I read all of it. Tests are the contract, and I want them failing before the change and passing after. When the output is wrong I don't argue with the model; I fix the brief, because a vague brief is usually my mistake. I keep a short list of things I still do by hand on purpose: naming, data models, anything that touches money or auth, and the final review before anything ships.

In practice that recently meant a feature spanning the web client, backend services and both mobile apps, shipped in three weeks by two people, with better test coverage than the code around it. I've shipped LLM-backed product features and know that side well. I haven't yet owned the model side, and I'd like to.
